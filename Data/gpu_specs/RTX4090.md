# RTX 4090

## Overview

The NVIDIA RTX 4090 is the flagship consumer GPU of the Ada Lovelace generation. It is designed for high-end gaming, AI workloads, content creation, rendering, and workstation-level productivity tasks. Due to its large VRAM capacity, high CUDA core count, and powerful Tensor Cores, it is one of the most capable consumer GPUs for local AI development.

---

## Specifications

Vendor: NVIDIA

Architecture: Ada Lovelace

Launch Year: 2022

VRAM: 24 GB GDDR6X

CUDA Cores: 16384

Tensor Cores: 512

RT Cores: 128

Memory Bandwidth: 1008 GB/s

TDP: 450W

PCIe Interface: PCIe 4.0 x16

---

## AI Workloads

### LLM Inference

The RTX 4090 is one of the best consumer GPUs for local LLM inference.

Suitable Models:

- Llama 7B
- Llama 8B
- Llama 13B
- Mistral 7B
- DeepSeek 7B
- Qwen 7B
- Gemma 7B

Can run many 30B-34B models when quantized.

70B models typically require aggressive quantization and offloading.

### Fine Tuning

Excellent for:

- LoRA fine tuning
- QLoRA
- PEFT workflows
- Small and medium-sized language models

### Stable Diffusion

Excellent performance for:

- Stable Diffusion XL
- Flux
- DreamBooth
- Image generation pipelines

### AI Training

Can train:

- Small neural networks
- Computer vision models
- Fine-tuned language models

Not ideal for training very large foundation models from scratch.

## Reasoning Facts

A GPU with 24 GB VRAM can run most 7B and 13B models comfortably.

24 GB VRAM is usually insufficient for FP16 70B models.

Tensor Cores accelerate AI workloads.

Memory bandwidth affects AI inference speed.

CUDA support improves compatibility with AI frameworks.

More VRAM generally improves LLM inference capability.

Ray tracing performance depends heavily on RT cores.

Stable Diffusion benefits from both VRAM and Tensor Cores.

The RTX 4090 is considered a flagship consumer GPU.
