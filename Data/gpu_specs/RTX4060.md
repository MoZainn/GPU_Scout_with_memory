# RTX 4060

## Overview

The NVIDIA RTX 4060 is a budget/entry-level GPU from the Ada Lovelace generation, aimed squarely at 1080p gaming and light content creation. It is one of the most commonly recommended cards for budget gaming builds due to its low power draw and price point.

---

## Specifications

Vendor: NVIDIA

Architecture: Ada Lovelace

Launch Year: 2023

VRAM: 8 GB GDDR6

CUDA Cores: 3072

Tensor Cores: 96

RT Cores: 24

Memory Bandwidth: 272 GB/s

TDP: 115W

PCIe Interface: PCIe 4.0 x8

---

## AI Workloads

### LLM Inference

8GB VRAM limits this card to small quantized models.

Suitable Models:

- Llama 7B (quantized, e.g. Q4)
- Mistral 7B (quantized)
- Small embedding models

Not suitable for 13B+ models without heavy offloading, which is slow.

### Stable Diffusion

Usable for SD 1.5 and SDXL, though 8GB VRAM means lower batch sizes and possible need for memory-efficient attention settings.

### Fine Tuning

Limited. QLoRA on small models is possible but constrained by VRAM.

---

## Gaming

### 1080p Gaming

This is the card's primary use case. Handles most current titles at high settings, 60+ FPS, with room for DLSS to push higher frame rates.

### 1440p Gaming

Workable at medium-high settings in most games, but the 8GB VRAM buffer can become a limiting factor in newer, texture-heavy titles before the GPU core itself does.

### 4K Gaming

Not recommended. Both VRAM and raw compute are undersized for native 4K.

### Ray Tracing

Entry-level RT performance. Light ray tracing effects are playable with DLSS enabled; heavier ray tracing (path tracing) is not practical at good frame rates.

---

## Power and System Requirements

Low power draw (115W) means this card works well in budget builds — often no separate PCIe power connector is required, or only a single 8-pin, depending on the specific model. A 550-650W PSU is comfortably sufficient for a full system built around this GPU.

---

## Value Positioning

Best suited for: budget-conscious 1080p gamers, HTPC/small form factor builds, and anyone prioritizing low power draw and quiet operation over raw performance. Not a good choice if 1440p or 4K gaming, or serious local AI work, is the primary goal — a 12GB card is a better long-term investment for those use cases.
