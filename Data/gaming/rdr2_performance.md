# Red Dead Redemption 2 — PC Performance and Requirements

## Overview

RDR2 released on PC in November 2019 and is now several years old, meaning most current and even several-generations-old GPUs handle it comfortably. It's a useful reference point for how Rockstar's RAGE engine performs, since GTA 6 will likely share architectural DNA with it.

## Official Rockstar Minimum Requirements

- CPU: Intel Core i5-2500K / AMD FX-6300
- GPU: NVIDIA GeForce GTX 770 2GB / AMD Radeon R9 280 2GB
- RAM: 8GB
- Storage: 150GB
- Result: Low settings, roughly 1080p 30 FPS

## Official Rockstar Recommended Requirements

- CPU: Intel Core i7-4770K / AMD Ryzen 5 1500X
- GPU: NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1060 6GB / AMD Radeon RX 480 4GB
- RAM: 12GB
- Storage: 150GB (SSD strongly recommended for load times)
- Result: Higher settings, smoother frame rates at 1080p

## How Modern Budget/Mid-Range GPUs Handle It

Since RDR2's baseline requirements are now several years old, current budget and mid-range GPUs all comfortably exceed the recommended spec:

- **RTX 3060 / RTX 4060**: High to Ultra settings at 1080p with high frame rates; solid 1440p at high settings.
- **RTX 4060 Ti (16GB) / RX 7600**: Comfortable 1440p at high settings, playable 4K with upscaling on lower presets.
- **RTX 4090 / RTX 5090**: Massive overkill for RDR2 — will run native 4K at maxed-out settings with high, stable frame rates. RDR2 has no ray tracing implementation, so flagship RT hardware isn't a factor here the way it is in newer titles.

## Practical Notes

- RDR2 does **not** support ray tracing — it predates widespread RT adoption, so RT core count is irrelevant for this specific game.
- The game is more VRAM-tolerant than many newer titles; even 6-8GB cards handle it well at 1080p/1440p.
- An SSD meaningfully improves load times given the 150GB install size and open-world streaming, though it's not a hard requirement.
