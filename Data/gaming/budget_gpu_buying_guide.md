# Budget Gaming GPU Buying Guide

## Philosophy

"Worth it" for a budget GPU means matching the card to your actual monitor resolution and target frame rate — buying more GPU than your monitor/CPU can use is wasted money, and buying less means constant compromise on settings.

## By Resolution Target

### 1080p, 60 FPS, competitive/esports titles
The RTX 4060, RTX 3060, or RX 7600 are all comfortably enough here. At this tier, a fast CPU matters at least as much as the GPU, since 1080p esports titles are frequently CPU-bound (see the hardware guide for details on CPU bottlenecks).

### 1080p, high settings, AAA single-player titles
RTX 4060 / RTX 3060 / RX 7600 are all solid picks. The RTX 3060's 12GB VRAM gives it a slight edge in newer, texture-heavy titles over the 8GB alternatives.

### 1440p, high settings
RTX 4060 Ti (16GB) is the strongest budget-tier pick here — the extra VRAM specifically helps at this resolution in modern titles. RX 7600 and plain RTX 4060 are workable but will need to drop some settings in the most demanding titles.

### 4K
None of the budget-tier cards above are genuinely built for native 4K. If 4K is the actual target, that pushes into a different (higher) price bracket entirely — a budget-tier answer isn't the right fit for that goal, and it's worth being upfront about that rather than stretching a budget card past its comfortable range.

## By Priority

**Best pure rasterized performance-per-dollar:** RX 7600 generally trades competitively with or slightly beats the RTX 4060 in games without heavy ray tracing.

**Best if you also want to try local AI:** RTX 3060 (12GB) or RTX 4060 Ti (16GB) — VRAM capacity matters more than raw gaming performance for local AI workloads, and both punch above their price class here.

**Best ray tracing at this price point:** RTX 4060 or RTX 4060 Ti — NVIDIA's RT hardware and DLSS upscaling remain ahead of AMD's equivalent tier as of this writing.

**Best value if buying used:** RTX 3060 (12GB) is frequently the best VRAM-per-dollar pick on the used market, since it was widely produced and its 12GB buffer has aged unusually well for its original price tier.

## What "Low Budget" Should NOT Mean

A genuinely low budget for gaming doesn't mean ignoring VRAM. An 8GB card that's cheap today can become a bottleneck in 2-3 years as games' texture budgets grow — the RTX 3060's 12GB at a similar or lower price point is often the smarter long-term buy than a newer but VRAM-constrained 8GB card. When budget GPUs are close in raw performance, VRAM capacity is usually the tiebreaker for how well the purchase ages.
