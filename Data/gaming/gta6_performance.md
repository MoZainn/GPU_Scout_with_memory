# GTA 6 — PC Performance and Requirements (Current Status)

## Important: No PC Version Is Confirmed

As of mid-2026, Rockstar Games has announced GTA 6 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S only, launching November 19, 2026. Rockstar has **not** announced a PC version, a PC release date, or official PC system requirements. Anything claiming to be "official GTA 6 PC specs" circulating online is a community estimate, not a Rockstar-published spec sheet.

Historical pattern: Rockstar's previous title, GTA V, launched on consoles in September 2013 and did not arrive on PC until April 2015 — roughly 18 months later. Red Dead Redemption 2 followed a similar pattern (consoles in 2018, PC in late 2019, about a year later). If GTA 6 follows the same pattern, a realistic PC release window is **2027-2028**, not alongside the console launch.

## What Can Be Reasonably Estimated

Since GTA 6 targets the PS5 and Xbox Series X/S as its baseline hardware, any eventual PC version's recommended specs will very likely aim to match or exceed that console hardware class:

- A modern multi-core CPU (8 cores recommended — Rockstar open worlds are historically CPU-heavy due to NPC/traffic/physics simulation, not just GPU-heavy)
- A current-generation GPU roughly in the RTX 4060/4070 class or better for 1080p-1440p at good settings
- Around 16GB of system RAM as a sensible baseline
- Fast NVMe SSD storage (Rockstar's asset streaming leans heavily on SSD speeds — RDR2 already recommended this)

These are reasoned estimates based on console hardware and Rockstar's history, **not official numbers**. Treat any specific FPS claim for GTA 6 (e.g. "RTX 4090 gets X FPS in GTA 6") as unverifiable speculation until Rockstar publishes real PC requirements — there is no way to benchmark a game that hasn't released on PC.

## Practical Answer for "Will GPU X Run GTA 6?"

The honest answer right now is: **we don't know, because there's no PC version to run.** The most useful thing to say is:

1. GTA 6 has no PC release date or specs yet.
2. Based on console hardware and Rockstar's history with GTA V and RDR2, a high-end current-gen GPU (RTX 4070 or better) is a safe bet for good settings once it does arrive.
3. A budget/mid-range GPU (RTX 4060, RTX 3060) will very likely run it at lower settings, similar to how those cards handle RDR2 today, but expect to lower settings on a demanding open-world title.
4. Revisit this question once Rockstar actually announces a PC version — check Rockstar's official support page directly for real requirements when that happens, rather than trusting predictive spec-sheet articles.
